How you compare

Where the household sits in each country: 75th percentile means richer than 75% of the group. Survey figures are brought to today's prices; the latest surveys date from 2022 to 2025.

How these are worked out
  • Wealth: net worth against households, all ages and the same age group (age of the main earner, here the oldest adult). Survey wealth leaves out pension rights, so the default here is without pensions; the US survey counts retirement accounts, so compare the US with pensions on.
  • Netherlands wealth is from CBS tax records, which also leave out cars. Other European countries are from the ECB household survey, which gives every tenth percentile for all households but only the median by age: there, the age-group percentile is an estimate that assumes the same spread as all households. US figures are from the Federal Reserve's Survey of Consumer Finances.
  • Income per person: your household's income over the last 12 budget months, after tax, divided by the standard scale (1 for the first adult, 0.5 for each other adult or child 14 and over, 0.3 per child under 14), against Eurostat's figures for everyone in the country. Not available for the US.
  • Household gross income and personal earnings need each adult's gross yearly income: ask Claude to add it. Earnings are compared with full-time employees (Eurostat or the US BLS); with only a few published points, those percentiles are estimates.
  • Your figures are converted at today's exchange rates, with no adjustment for differences in prices between countries. A person's view compares their own wealth (joint money split as set) with households, since no survey measures individuals' wealth.

    Changing your goals

    • Ask Claude in any chat, for example make my savings goal 25%, add a goal of 500k net worth by 2032 or count Gym as a need. The change shows here right away.
    • Budget goals: needs, wants or savings, as a share of income or an amount a month, optionally from a start month and with a deadline.
    • Milestones: a target for net worth, invested assets, total assets, cash, debt, or months of spending held in cash, with an optional deadline.
    • The same way you can change the projection's returns and monthly saving, how joint accounts are split, and the countries you compare with.
